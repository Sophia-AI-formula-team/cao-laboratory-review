Research area

Autonomous Vehicle Motion Control

01

Control methods for automated driving, including cooperative highway merging and coordinated vehicle motion.

Status
Active research
Period
Ongoing
Main topics
Model predictive control · Optimal control · Vehicle dynamics

Overview

What we study

This area applies control theory to the motion of automated vehicles. Current themes include generating smooth, collision-free merging maneuvers and coordinating vehicles under changing traffic conditions.

Problem we are addressing

An automated vehicle must plan and execute safe motion while respecting physical limits, nearby traffic, and the need to compute a response in real time.

Approach

How we work

  1. A

    Model the system

    Represent vehicle motion, constraints, and interactions in a form suitable for controller design.

  2. B

    Formulate the objective

    Express safety, travel time, traffic flow, and energy use as explicit control objectives.

  3. C

    Compute control actions

    Use optimal and model predictive control to generate feasible actions as conditions change.

  4. D

    Verify performance

    Evaluate the controller in simulation and experiments, including difficult and mixed-traffic cases.

Research directions

Research directions

  • Control strategies for cooperative automated merging
  • Vehicle-group control with explicit safety and stability objectives
  • Evaluation methods that connect controller design to traffic and energy outcomes
Next step

Current work continues to study robust real-time control when surrounding traffic does not behave exactly as predicted.
